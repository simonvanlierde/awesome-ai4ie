This map shows where AI is applied in industrial ecology, along **two
dimensions**: the IE field (LCA, MFA, waste & circularity, …) and the AI technique
(LLMs, computer vision, tabular ML, …). Every entry links to the
[literature list](/literature/) pre-filtered to the same tags, so the map is also an
index into the reading list.

## What works today, per field

One section per field: a paragraph on what works today, then the field's
applications, each with a maturity call. Open an application for its description and
the papers behind it, or see every paper in the [literature list](/literature/).

### LCA & footprinting

Two things demonstrably work. The first is **tabular ML** for rapid impact screening
and inventory gap-filling, validated repeatedly since 2017. The second is **LLM
extraction** of inventory data from literature and reports: working systems reach
F1 ≈ 0.82–0.86 <Cite id="kumarLargeLanguageModelbased2025" />, always with human verification. The consensus rule across this work: use LLMs to *extract*
inventory data, never to generate it <Cite id="preussLargeLanguageModels2024" />. A 2025 benchmark found 37% of general-purpose
LLM answers to LCA questions inaccurate or misleading, with hallucinated-citation
rates up to 40% <Cite id="donaldsonExpertGroundedBenchmark2025" />. Automated footprinting deploys at catalog scale for common products <Cite id="balajiFlamingoEnvironmentalImpact2023" />
and degrades for products outside that set. [Papers →](/literature/?ie=lca)

### MFA & stocks and flows

The most mature corner of the map. Satellite-based material stock mapping works at
**national scale (10 m)** <Cite id="haberlHighResolutionMaps2021" /> from free data, and a **global estimate (90 m, 547 Gt in
buildings)** <Cite id="haberlGlobalBuiltEnvironment2024" /> exists. Mind the validation spread: 53–200% against independent
studies. That spread is the real accuracy ceiling. Night-lights proxies <Cite id="peledSpatialMaterialStock2021" /> and deep multi-source
fusion <Cite id="baoDeepLearningFusion2023" /> improve on single sources but still need ground data. Street-view CV for
building attributes is demonstrated feasibility: good in the training city, sharply
worse elsewhere <Cite id="huangImageBasedPrediction2024" />. [Papers →](/literature/?ie=mfa)

### Waste, EoL & circularity

The corner with the most publications. Task-tuned detection and counting of waste and
components is well-validated <Cite id="majchrowskaDeepLearningbasedWaste2022" />. Camera-based flow characterization runs in operating
WEEE sorting plants <Cite id="vogelgesangAutomatedMaterialFlow2025" />. Label-OCR identification is deployed in pilots, where readability
collapses from 96% in repair shops to 40% at the recycler <Cite id="sterkensProductLabelIdentification2023" />. Worn, dirty and damaged
products are the recurring failure mode. Foundation models (<Cite id="kirillovSegmentAnything2023">SAM</Cite>, <Cite id="radfordLearningTransferableVisual2021">CLIP</Cite>) demo
impressively. They still need a class-assignment layer and sorting-grade evaluation
before they count as methods.
Robotic disassembly works for narrow product families <Cite id="ameurFutureRoboticDisassembly2025" />.
[Papers →](/literature/?ie=eol) · Full depth in the
[CV taxonomy worked example](/applications-map/cv-taxonomy/).

## Not mapped yet

The gaps in this map matter as much as the filled cells, so we name them here
instead of leaving them implicit. Two kinds of gap: **IE fields** with no vetted entries, and
**AI techniques** with no tag in the vocabulary yet.

A caveat on how to read this: none of it claims the work doesn't exist. It claims we
have not vetted any of it. Our sweep either found nothing that survived verification,
or never looked. Each line is the easiest possible contribution: one paper, one entry.

### IE fields with no entries

- <span class="wanted">Wanted</span> **Chemicals, toxicity & QSAR.** Predicting properties and toxicity for substances
  nobody has tested, where LCA needs characterisation factors for tens of thousands of them.
  Plausibly the largest single hole in this map.
- <span class="wanted">Wanted</span> **Energy systems & industrial decarbonisation.** Marginal versus average grid
  factors, temporal accounting, process and heat-integration optimisation.
- <span class="wanted">Wanted</span> **Industrial symbiosis.** Matching one plant's waste to another's input is a
  recommender problem wearing an IE name.
- <span class="wanted">Wanted</span> **Supply chains, criticality & resource security.** Supply risk, critical raw
  materials, trade-network disruption.
- <span class="wanted">Wanted</span> **Agriculture, food & land use.** The field our remote-sensing tag most obviously
  serves, with nothing behind it.
- <span class="wanted">Wanted</span> **Product lifetime, reuse & repair.** Survival analysis and failure prediction;
  feeds both MFA stock models and the EoL corner.
- <span class="wanted">Wanted</span> **Water.** Footprint, scarcity, and hydrological modelling.
- <span class="wanted">Wanted</span> **Input–output analysis, forecasting & scenario modelling.** The original three
  gaps, still open.

### AI techniques with no tag

Tags are added together with their first entry, so these stay out of the vocabulary until
someone brings a paper that earns one.

- <span class="wanted">Wanted</span> **Uncertainty quantification & Bayesian methods.** Uncertainty is this discipline's
  central methodological concern, and the map has no column for it. Conformal prediction counts too.
- <span class="wanted">Wanted</span> **Graph neural networks & network analysis.** Supply chains, IO tables, material-flow
  networks, molecular graphs: most IE data is a network, and nothing here treats it as one.
- <span class="wanted">Wanted</span> **Surrogate models & emulators.** Physics-informed ones included. This is how
  scenario work gets done with ML in practice, not just in principle.
- <span class="wanted">Wanted</span> **Causal inference.** Rebound effects and policy attribution are causal claims,
  usually made with correlational tools.
- <span class="wanted">Wanted</span> **Reinforcement learning.** Disassembly sequence planning and process control. The
  EoL paragraph above already discusses robotic disassembly with no technique tag behind it.
- <span class="wanted">Wanted</span> **Knowledge graphs & ontologies.** LCI interoperability and semantic flow matching,
  distinct from NLP extraction.
- <span class="wanted">Wanted</span> **Recommender & matching systems.** The method behind industrial symbiosis, and
  behind matching products to emission factors.
- <span class="wanted">Wanted</span> **Time-series & forecasting.** Distinct from tabular ML.
- <span class="wanted">Wanted</span> **Federated & privacy-preserving learning.** [Local AI & hardware](/tools/local-ai/)
  argues for keeping sensitive data in-house; this is how institutions learn from data they
  are never going to pool.
- <span class="wanted">Wanted</span> **Explainability.** Whether someone can interrogate a claim from a model-based assessment.

### Where the gaps meet

Several of these are not separate holes but one cell each: **chemicals × graph neural
networks**, **scenarios × surrogates**, **symbiosis × matching**, **EoL × reinforcement
learning**. Those four are the most concrete places to start, because each names both a
field and a method rather than a vague area.
