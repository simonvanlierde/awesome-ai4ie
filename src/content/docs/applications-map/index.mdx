This map shows where AI is applied in industrial ecology, along **two
dimensions**: the IE field and the AI technique (LLMs, computer vision, tabular ML, …).
The five fields follow the [subject sections](https://is4ie.org/sections) of the
International Society for Industrial Ecology (ISIE): life cycle assessment,
socio-economic metabolism, input-output, circular economy and industrial symbiosis. Every entry links to the
[literature list](/literature/) pre-filtered to the same tags, so the map is also an
index into the reading list. The list has a third facet the map leaves out: the
**topic**, the system a paper is about (chemicals, water, buildings, …). A topic cuts
across fields, so it gets a filter but no section of its own.

## What works today, per field

One section per field: a paragraph on what works today, then the field's
applications, each with a maturity call. Open an application for its description and
the papers behind it, or see every paper in the [literature list](/literature/).

### Life cycle assessment

Two things demonstrably work. The first is **tabular ML** for rapid impact screening
and inventory gap-filling, validated repeatedly since 2017. The second is **LLM
extraction** of inventory data from literature and reports: working systems reach
an F1 score of 0.82–0.86 (1 would mean every value found and none wrong) <Cite id="kumarLargeLanguageModelbased2025" />, with a person checking the values. The papers here use LLMs to *extract*
inventory data, not to generate it <Cite id="preussLargeLanguageModels2024" />. A 2025 benchmark found 37% of general-purpose
LLM answers to LCA questions inaccurate or misleading, with hallucinated-citation
rates up to 40% <Cite id="donaldsonExpertGroundedBenchmark2025" />. Automated footprinting works for products with a clear match in the emission-factor database. In one evaluation, a few wrong matches caused most of the error <Cite id="balajiFlamingoEnvironmentalImpact2023" />.
A model that predicts construction products' footprints from their declarations
reports its own uncertainty: low between 3 and 500 kg CO₂e, significant below 3 kg. That uncertainty
covers the model, not the declarations it learned from <Cite id="baehrPredictingProductLife2024" />. [Papers →](/literature/?ie=lca)

### Socio-economic metabolism

Socio-economic metabolism covers material flow analysis (MFA) and the stock models built on
it ([Pauliuk & Hertwich, 2015](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ecolecon.2015.08.012)). Satellite stock mapping is the furthest along here. It works at
**national scale (10 m)** <Cite id="haberlHighResolutionMaps2021" /> from free data, and a **global estimate (90 m, 547 Gt in
buildings)** <Cite id="haberlGlobalBuiltEnvironment2024" /> exists. Against independent
studies, results range from 53% to 200%. Neither side is ground truth, so treat that
spread as the uncertainty. Night-lights proxies <Cite id="peledSpatialMaterialStock2021" /> and deep multi-source
fusion <Cite id="baoDeepLearningFusion2023" /> improve on single sources but still need ground data. Street-view CV for
building attributes is demonstrated feasibility: good in the training city, sharply
worse elsewhere <Cite id="huangImageBasedPrediction2024" />.

**Bayesian MFA** is the most developed uncertainty thread. It combines expert-elicited
priors with noisy data <Cite id="dongExpertElicitationData2023" />, scales to disaggregated
systems with around 180 flow and stock variables <Cite id="wangBayesianMaterialFlow2024" />,
and can rank which flow is worth measuring next <Cite id="liaoIntelligentDataCollection2025" />.
Applied to England's construction aggregates, it quantifies 146 flows and stock changes from reported
data that cover 20% of the system <Cite id="masonBayesianMaterialFlow2025" />.
[Papers →](/literature/?ie=sem) · [Uncertainty papers →](/literature/?ml=uncertainty)

### Circular economy

The corner with the most publications. Detecting and counting waste and components is
accurate on the benchmarks a model was tuned for, and untested on field photos <Cite id="majchrowskaDeepLearningbasedWaste2022" />. On a pilot-scale
industrial sorter, cameras estimated the composition of shredded e-waste (WEEE) from recorded data; no plant runs it yet <Cite id="vogelgesangAutomatedMaterialFlow2025" />. Reading product labels with text recognition (OCR) is deployed in pilots, where readability
collapses from 96% in repair shops to 40% at the recycler <Cite id="sterkensProductLabelIdentification2023" />. Worn, dirty and damaged
products are the recurring failure mode. Foundation models (<Cite id="kirillovSegmentAnything2023">SAM</Cite>, <Cite id="radfordLearningTransferableVisual2021">CLIP</Cite>) demo
impressively. They still need a class-assignment layer and sorting-grade evaluation
before they count as methods.
Robotic disassembly works for narrow product families <Cite id="ameurFutureRoboticDisassembly2025" />.
[Papers →](/literature/?ie=circular) · Full depth in the
[CV taxonomy worked example](/applications-map/cv-taxonomy/).

### Industrial symbiosis

In industrial symbiosis, matching one plant's waste to another's input is, in part, a
recommender problem, and most of the work so far mines text for the matches. LLMs can build a waste-to-resource
knowledge graph from the literature; one recovered 24 of 45 known exchanges in the Taranto
district <Cite id="zhaoConstructionWastetoresourceKnowledge2025" />. Word vectors trained on
patents and papers rank substitutes well at some eco-industrial parks and poorly at others,
Kalundborg among them <Cite id="davisMachineLearningassistedIndustrial2022" />. Tagging
free-text waste descriptions with European Waste Catalogue codes stays hard: top-10
precision is 0.36 for plain term matching and 0.39 at best <Cite id="vancapelleveenBuildingRecommenderSystems2021" />.
Recommenders built on past exchange cases have been demonstrated on single firms <Cite id="grimmelUrbanIndustrialSymbiosis2025" />.
A language-model classifier trained on ecoinvent predicts which wastes an activity produces or needs.
Its F1 is above 0.82 on held-out ecoinvent data but 0.63 to 0.73 on 40 real exchanges <Cite id="zhaoNovelMachineLearningbased2025" />.

## Not mapped yet

The gaps in this map matter as much as the filled cells, so we name them here
instead of leaving them implicit. Three kinds of gap: an **ISIE section** with no field here
yet, **topics** with fewer than three entries, and **AI techniques** with no tag in the
vocabulary yet.

A caveat on how to read this: none of it claims the work doesn't exist. It claims we
have not vetted any of it. Our sweep either found nothing that survived verification,
or never looked. Each line is the easiest possible contribution: one paper, one entry. Its
link opens a short form on GitHub with the gap filled in.

### Where the gaps meet

Several of these are not separate holes but one cell each: **chemicals × graph neural
networks**, **scenarios × surrogates**, **symbiosis × matching**, **circular economy ×
reinforcement learning**. Those four are the most concrete places to start, because each names both a
field and a method rather than a vague area.
