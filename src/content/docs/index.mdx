## Who this is for

- **An LCA or MFA researcher.** Start with [what works in your field](/applications-map/#what-works-today-per-field), then the [ML-reproducibility checklist](/best-practices/checklists/#machine-learning-reproducibility) before your first model. *Contribute:* examples of AI in your field.
- **A student.** Start with [getting started](/best-practices/) and the [ML landmarks](/literature/?type=landmark-ml) reading list. *Contribute:* the pitfall you wish someone had warned you about.
- **A research software engineer (RSE) or tool builder.** Start with [tools & hardware](/tools/) and the [how-to-add-a-demo guide](/demos/#two-ways-to-add-a-demo). *Contribute:* a demo of your method, embedded or in-browser.
- **Anyone else.** *Contribute:* an answer to any <span class="wanted">Wanted</span> mark on the site (each gap on the map links to a short form), or a pull request for any other change.

## Contributing

No pull request needed: [suggest a paper](repo:issues/new?template=paper.yml),
[map an application](repo:issues/new?template=application.yml),
[suggest a tool](repo:issues/new?template=tool.yml) or
[suggest anything else](repo:issues/new?template=other.yml). Each is a short form on
GitHub, and a maintainer turns it into an entry.

To make the change yourself, the [contributing guide](repo:blob/main/CONTRIBUTING.md)
explains the format and where each type of entry goes. The data lives in two YAML
files, and every page has an edit link.
