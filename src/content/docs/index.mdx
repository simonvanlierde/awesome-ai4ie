Industrial ecology studies how materials and energy move through the economy: what
products are made of, how long they last, where they end up, and what that costs the
environment. AI can help with a lot of that work, and it can also produce confident
nonsense. This guide sorts the two and points to the papers behind each verdict.

It's community-curated, started in the **AI4IE working group**: IE researchers
scattered across institutes who meet on [Discord](https://discord.gg/zhHAZakXHX).
Anyone can contribute.

## Contributing

What helps most: examples of AI in your field, the pitfall you wish someone had warned you
about, a demo of your method (embedded or in-browser), or an answer to any
<span class="wanted">Wanted</span> mark on the site (each gap on the map links to a short form).

No pull request needed: [suggest a paper](repo:issues/new?template=paper.yml),
[map an application](repo:issues/new?template=application.yml),
[suggest a tool](repo:issues/new?template=tool.yml),
[suggest a course, video or post](repo:issues/new?template=resource.yml) or
[suggest anything else](repo:issues/new?template=other.yml). Each is a short form on
GitHub, and a maintainer turns it into an entry. The forms need a free GitHub account.
Without one, post your suggestion in the [Discord](https://discord.gg/zhHAZakXHX).

To make the change yourself, the [contributing guide](repo:blob/main/CONTRIBUTING.md)
explains the format and where each type of entry goes. The data lives in three YAML
files, and every page has an edit link.
