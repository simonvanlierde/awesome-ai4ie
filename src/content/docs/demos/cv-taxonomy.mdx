This page grades what computer vision can measure in discarded products, components and
materials, and how well each method has been tested. It is a draft summary of the
full taxonomy, [What can a machine actually see?](https://simonvanlierde.com/cv-ie-taxonomy/)

## Taxonomy at a glance

Structure means the bill of components (BoC), which lists a product's parts and how they join.
Other abbreviations: BoM (bill of materials), OCR (optical character recognition: reading text
from images), VLM (vision-language model: answers questions about images), WEEE (waste
electrical and electronic equipment) and mAP50 (detection accuracy, from 0 to 1 or as a percentage).

P = partial, E = emerging but narrow, U = plausible but unvalidated (details below the table).

<p class="table-title"><b>Table 5.1</b> What computer vision can read from a discarded product</p>

| Scale ↓ / Info type → | Identity | Quantity | Structure (BoC) | Condition |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Product** | Label OCR + retrieval (**P**) | Depth / multi-view geometry (**P**) | Not sensed | Wear / damage grading (**E**) |
| **Component** | Marking OCR + open-vocab naming (**P**) | Per-part geometry, occluded (**P**) | Detection (**P**) / attachment relations (**E**) | Connector-state grading (**U**) |
| **Material** | Surface BoM classification (**P**); full BoM an open gap | Derived (geometry × density) | Not sensed | Corrosion / contamination (**U**) |

**Maturity grades,** from highest to lowest:

- **Strong:** works across many kinds of product on a benchmark, works on field photos, and is used in practice. No cell reaches this yet.
- **P, partial:** works across many kinds of product on a benchmark, but fails on field photos or is not yet used in practice.
- **E, emerging-but-narrow:** works only for one narrow kind of product.
- **U, plausible-but-unvalidated:** shown only in principle or in a neighbouring field.
- *Not sensed* or *Derived:* no method reads it from a photo; at best it is computed from other cells.

## Product-level tasks

- **Identity (partial).** Software reads the label text, then matches it to a reference
  database. Image retrieval and VLMs can help. On washing machines, it read model numbers in
  95.7% of usable repair photos but 39.7% of usable recycler photos
  <Cite id="sterkensProductLabelIdentification2023" />. Labels wear out at end of life, and
  the database may not list the model.
- **Quantity (partial, approximate).** Three families of method recover geometry with
  error bounds: metric depth from one photo (<Cite id="huMetric3DV22024">Metric3D v2</Cite>, <Cite id="bochkovskiiDepthProSharp2024">Depth Pro</Cite>,
  <Cite id="yangDepthAnythingV22024">Depth Anything v2</Cite>), feed-forward multi-view geometry (<Cite id="wangVGGTVisualGeometry2025">VGGT</Cite>, <Cite id="keethaMapAnythingUniversalFeedForward2025">MapAnything</Cite>), and
  photogrammetry / <Cite id="mildenhallNeRFRepresentingScenes2020">NeRF</Cite> / <Cite id="kerbl3DGaussianSplatting2023">3D Gaussian splatting</Cite>.
  Mass estimation from a single image exists but is coarse. All of them see visible surfaces
  only. Reflective, transparent and thin parts are the hardest cases.
- **Condition (emerging but narrow).** Wear, damage, and corrosion recognition feeding
  repair / reuse / recycle triage. Per-unit grading works for single product classes on
  fixed camera rigs, such as phone buyback kiosks and laptop-cover inspection. None is
  validated across product classes or on field photos. Zero-shot anomaly detection
  (<Cite id="jeongWinCLIPZeroFewShot2023">WinCLIP</Cite>, <Cite id="zhouAnomalyCLIPObjectAgnostic2023">AnomalyCLIP</Cite>) needs no training images of the product
  and is more general. But it flags that something is wrong, not how worn a unit is.

## Component-level tasks

- **Structure / BoC (partial detection, emerging-but-narrow relations).** YOLOv8
  segmented laptop components at 98.8% mAP50 on a test set of 56 images, drawn from
  560 photos of three MacBook models <Cite id="zhangEvaluatingLargeLightweight2026" />. That study used
  controlled photos. Segmenters such as <Cite id="kirillovSegmentAnything2023">SAM</Cite>
  outline regions, so identifying parts needs information about their class.
  Single-photo methods such as <Cite id="liuSINGAPOSingleImage2025">SINGAPO</Cite> and
  <Cite id="chenURDFormerPipelineConstructing2024">URDFormer</Cite> recover movable joints
  for some household objects. A disassembly list also needs to record fasteners and
  connections such as screws, glue and clips.
- **Identity (partial).** OCR on component markings plus one-shot / retrieval recognition and
  open-vocabulary naming from a text prompt. Fine-grained brand- or part-specific identity is
  not reliably recovered.
- **Quantity (partial, approximate).** Inherits the product-level geometry methods,
  aggravated by occlusion within the assembled product.
- **Condition (plausible but unvalidated).** Connector-state recognition (screws, clips,
  solder joints) for disassembly planning. Published work shows fastener *detection*, not
  per-unit condition *grading*.

## Material-level tasks

- **Identity / BoM (partial on visible surfaces, open gap for full BoM).** Methods classify
  and segment the material of *visible* surfaces. Examples are <Cite id="bellMaterialRecognitionWild2015">MINC</Cite>'s
  23-class in-the-wild baseline, one- or few-shot similarity given a region mask, and
  <Cite id="radfordLearningTransferableVisual2021">CLIP</Cite>-based recognition. CLIP is
  fragile when photos differ from its training data and when a part mixes materials. Only
  one study measures composition from images: a pilot-scale sorter for shredded WEEE, using
  <Cite id="redmonYouOnlyLook2015">YOLO</Cite> (mAP50 ≈ 0.99 on recorded data)
  <Cite id="vogelgesangAutomatedMaterialFlow2025" />. It measures a mixed flow at a plant, not
  one intact product. The composition of hidden interior parts is an open gap.
- **Quantity (derived, not sensed).** Per-material mass or volume would come from component
  geometry × material class × a density prior. No dedicated method exists, and the estimate
  compounds the uncertainty of both inputs.
- **Condition (plausible but unvalidated).** Corrosion, degradation, and contamination.
  Surface segmentation can isolate regions, but per-region grading on arbitrary products is
  not validated.
